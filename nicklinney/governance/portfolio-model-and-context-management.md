# Portfolio Model and Context Management

## Purpose

This page defines the conceptual model, boundaries, and operating discipline for managing projects, products, and resources under the NickLinneyDev portfolio.

Its role is to establish:

- clear separation of concerns
- what qualifies as portfolio-owned
- how identity, namespace, and attribution are maintained
- how growth is made deliberate instead of accidental

## Portfolio Model

NickLinneyDev operates as a portfolio root rather than merely a repository or brand label.

The portfolio is composed of:

- identity
- namespaces
- managed projects
- managed products
- supporting resources
- external knowledge sources

Each of these must be explicitly classified and governed.

## Foundational Separations

### Identity vs Infrastructure

Identity defines who owns and authors work. Infrastructure defines where and how work runs.

Infrastructure ownership does not imply portfolio ownership.

### Brand Portfolio vs Personal Projects

Portfolio objects are intentionally created, deliberately named, explicitly documented, and eligible for public attribution.

Personal projects may inform portfolio work, but do not inherit branding or attribution automatically.

### Conceptual Frameworks vs Implementations

Conceptual systems and concrete implementations are distinct.

Implementations must reference their governing concept.

### Namespaces vs Artifacts

Namespaces define ownership, authority, and scope.

Artifacts exist within namespaces, not alongside them.

Namespace decisions precede artifact creation.

### Knowledge Flow vs Attribution

Knowledge may flow across domains, but attribution does not automatically follow.

## Context Management Rules

Every project or product must answer:

- What is its parent namespace?
- What portfolio object authorizes its existence?
- Where is its authoritative documentation?

If any of these are unclear, the object is out of compliance.

## Documentation Hierarchy

The governing hierarchy is:

1. Portfolio governance
2. Namespace and ownership
3. Project-level documents
4. Product-level documents
5. Operational and implementation notes

Lower tiers must not contradict higher tiers.
