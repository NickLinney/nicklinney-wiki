# Good Faith Diligence Rule

## Purpose

This rule establishes a baseline expectation that each participant in the organization acts in good faith, performs reasonable due diligence, and verifies the elements of a problem or decision appropriate to their role and perspective.

## Part 1: Good Faith and Due Diligence

Each person is encouraged to:

- examine within their role and level of ownership
- consider the company-wide visibility level of the components they understand
- make the best decisions they can in good faith
- perform the due diligence they believe is required to complete the task appropriately

The recovered source frames this as a principle of thoughtful responsibility at both the individual and organizational level.

## Part 2: Trust but Verify

Participants should:

- trust colleagues to apply the Good Faith Diligence Rule to the best of their ability
- act as stewards of mutual responsibility for accuracy and competence
- verify the components that should be checked from their own perspective
- apply their own security and business-ethics judgment where relevant

This makes the rule both cooperative and supervisory: trust is preserved, but verification remains an obligation.

## Operational Interpretation

The rule implies that:

- no one should act carelessly merely because another person has already reviewed something
- verification is role-sensitive rather than universally identical
- diligence should be documented when the decision is consequential
- organizational accuracy is a shared responsibility

## Governance Value

This rule helps create:

- mutual accountability
- better decision quality
- better ethical stewardship
- stronger competence signals
- more resilient governance under uncertainty
