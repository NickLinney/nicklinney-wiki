# Namespace Ownership and DNS Registration

## Purpose

This page defines how the NickLinneyDev namespace functions as a brand, portfolio namespace, and communication identifier.

It also preserves the critical distinction between namespace authority and physical or legal ownership of infrastructure.

## Core Position

NickLinneyDev is simultaneously:

- a publicly used moniker and brand identity
- a portfolio and product namespace
- a communication identifier used across web, documentation, and project references

However, it does not automatically imply independent ownership of physical assets.

## Ownership vs Representation

The governing distinction is:

- assets are owned by Nicholas Linney as an individual
- NickLinneyDev operates as a representational and organizational layer over those assets

This separation supports:

- operational clarity
- legal and liability hygiene
- future scalability

## DNS as Namespace Authority

Registered domains act as semantic and naming roots rather than as proof of hardware ownership.

Examples documented in the source material include:

- `nicklinney.com`
- `nicklinneysystem.com`
- `nicklinneysystem.net`
- `nicklinney.cloud`

The role of these domains is to anchor identity and namespace authority across public systems.

## Namespace Layering Model

```text
Person (Nicholas Linney)
|
+-- Physical Devices
+-- Personal Network
+-- Hosting Accounts
|
+-- NickLinneyDev
    +-- Public Brand Identity
    +-- Project and Product Registry
    +-- Documentation and Communication
    +-- DNS Namespace Authority
```

## Operational Implications

- NickLinneyDev is a public brand namespace, not a legal entity
- DNS registrations establish naming authority
- infrastructure attribution remains personal until explicitly reassigned
- brand presence and asset ownership must not be conflated
