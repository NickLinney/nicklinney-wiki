# Executive and Cabinet Meeting Record Standard

## Status

- Status: Canonical
- Canonicality: Canonical
- Origin: Wiki-native normalized control artifact derived from retrieved source-backed reporting governance
- Created: July 27, 2026

## Purpose

This standard defines how executive, cabinet, and governance meeting records should be captured so that the wiki preserves not only outputs, but also decision lineage.

It exists to support:

- institutional memory
- executive traceability
- decision compression across reporting layers
- consistent meeting-to-record publication discipline
- historical retention of strategic governance activity

## Governing Principle

Meeting records are not transcripts by default.

They are controlled governance artifacts that preserve:

- meeting identity
- purpose and scope
- attendees and accountable roles
- decisions taken
- unresolved issues
- linked reports and evidence
- follow-up actions
- publication and retention status

## Meeting Classes

| Meeting Class | Typical Cadence | Typical Audience | Primary Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Department Operational Review | Weekly | Supervisors and Directors | Operational status summary and escalations |
| Cabinet Review | Monthly | Cabinet leads and directors | Cabinet report and decision record |
| Executive Strategy Review | Quarterly | Executive leadership | Strategic review package and decisions |
| Annual Enterprise Review | Annual | Executive and ownership stakeholders | State of the enterprise record |

## Minimum Record Structure

Each meeting record should contain at least:

1. Record identifier and title.
2. Meeting class and cadence.
3. Meeting date and governing period.
4. Convener and accountable owner.
5. Attendee roles.
6. Inputs reviewed.
7. Decisions made.
8. Risks, exceptions, or escalations.
9. Follow-up actions and owners.
10. Linked downstream publications.
11. Retention and supersession notes.

## Publication Rules

- Tactical detail stays closest to operational ownership.
- Cabinet and executive records should summarize, not duplicate, underlying operational reports.
- Significant decisions should link to the reports, standards, ADRs, or registers they affect.
- If a meeting produces a durable policy, standard, or strategic decision, that derivative artifact should be published as its own controlled page.

## Knowledge Management Controls

Meeting records with strategic, governance, or policy significance should be entered into the enterprise document register or an approved companion register.

Records should carry enough metadata to support:

- approval tracing
- historical retrieval
- supersession tracking
- relationship mapping to policies, standards, and plans
