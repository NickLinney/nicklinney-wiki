## Official Motions and Commentary

### Item 1 - Entry into Execution Phase

Motion:

`Move that the initiative enter the execution phase under CIO leadership and PMO delivery governance, using the completed design corpus as the controlling design baseline and the CVO directive as authorization to produce implementation-facing artifacts.`

Commentary:

- Chief Information Officer: supported the motion and stated that the design corpus is sufficient to begin bounded execution planning without claiming final enterprise rollout readiness.
- Chief Information Security Officer: supported the motion and clarified that execution must begin with technology and control choices that preserve trust boundaries rather than compress them for convenience.
- PMO Director: supported the motion and stated that the execution phase should be treated as artifact-producing work, not merely a status change.
- Director of Product Management: supported the motion and added that the product plan already points toward implementation sequencing and pilot selection, so the motion is timely.
- Director of Information Technology: supported the motion and warned that execution must remain scoped to an operable first slice.
- Director of Information Security: supported the motion and stressed that the first execution artifacts must include approval and credential governance rather than infrastructure alone.
- Vice President of Enterprise Architecture: supported the motion and noted that the initiative now needs a first selected pattern, not more abstract option space.
- Director of DevOps: supported the motion and added that the execution gate should explicitly include a machine-path pilot and not only a human request path.
- Documentation Manager: supported the motion and confirmed that Knowledge Management can keep the execution corpus visible and coherent.

Disposition:

- Carried.

### Item 2 - Required Execution Outputs

Motion:

`Move that the execution-entry cycle produce, at minimum, a technical action plan, a technology comparison and recommendation artifact, a draft approval matrix, an initial implementation slice plan, and a mobilization meeting record.`

Commentary:

- Chief Information Officer: supported the motion and stated that this directly addresses the gap identified by the CVO.
- Chief Information Security Officer: supported the motion and clarified that the comparison artifact must challenge convenience-biased assumptions.
- PMO Director: supported the motion and stated that these outputs map cleanly to the project plan's execution-phase commitments.
- Director of Product Management: supported the motion and added that the implementation slice plan is the key artifact preventing execution drift.
- Director of Information Technology: supported the motion and warned against including too many platform-adjacent deliverables in the first cycle.
- Director of Information Security: supported the motion and stated that an approval matrix is necessary before infrastructure activation claims are meaningful.
- Vice President of Enterprise Architecture: supported the motion and added that the comparison artifact must explicitly preserve the federated service pattern.
- Director of DevOps: supported the motion and clarified that the technical action plan should include machine credential lifecycle steps.
- Documentation Manager: supported the motion and confirmed that each output can be published without exposing sensitive payloads.

Disposition:

- Carried.
