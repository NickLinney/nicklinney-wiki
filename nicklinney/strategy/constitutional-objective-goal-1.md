## Objective

Build an ontologically sound, self-improving cybernetic software ecosystem whose architecture, governance, automation, security, and AI behaviors are designed according to the Principle of Maximum Risk.

Under this principle, every component is assessed according to the highest credible harm it could enable rather than merely its intended use.

## Strategic Interpretation

The recovered source frames this not as ordinary software development, but as the governance of a safety-critical, security-critical, AI-mediated infrastructure system.

## Core Alignment Domains

### Ontology and Architecture

The system should preserve:

- deterministic domain models
- explicit relationships
- bounded contexts
- traceable design rationale

### Cybernetics and Self-Improvement

Feedback loops should operate through governed observation, proposal, review, and controlled change.

### Security

The recovered source emphasizes:

- critical-infrastructure baseline posture
- zero trust
- auditable supply chain
- secrets isolation

### Functional Safety and High-Assurance Governance

The objective aligns the ecosystem with safety lifecycle thinking, hazard analysis, defined safety functions, fail-safe behavior, and rigorous review expectations.

### AI Safety and Dual-Use Governance

The objective requires AI systems, tools, and capabilities to be governed as potentially high-consequence, dual-use, or emergent-risk systems where appropriate.

### Ethics

The recovered source emphasizes:

- safety
- consent
- auditability
- reversibility
- least privilege
- human accountability by design

## Strategic Layering Implication

## Governance Implication

The objective implies that the ecosystem should be treated as a governed cybernetic infrastructure whose capabilities must remain subordinate to assurance, security, auditability, and human authority.
