## Executive Summary

The COO retrospective identifies the need for an operations service catalog and runtime evidence model because the enterprise has real operating cadence without a unified operational baseline across operations, environment support, and hosting. This initiative is therefore framed as a bounded evidence-building program that can strengthen runtime honesty without implying that hosting maturity or enterprise reliability has already been achieved.

## Initiative Purpose

The purpose of the initiative is to define and govern a practical operational service baseline for the enterprise by establishing:

- named service ownership across operations, environment support, and hosting
- support, escalation, and review expectations for each governed service
- runtime evidence requirements for uptime, deployment, backup, and recovery claims
- recurring KPI, KRI, and action-register review discipline

## Why This Initiative Now

The closed ECI corpus shows that `NickLinney.Ops` and `NickLinney.env` are real in narrow scope while `NickLinney.InfrastructureHosting` remains under-realized. That combination creates a governance opportunity and a risk: the enterprise has enough operating reality to define honest service boundaries, but not enough evidence to support broader reliability claims. A bounded service-catalog and runtime-evidence initiative addresses that gap directly.

## Initial Deliverables

- executive abstract
- controlled service catalog with named owners
- runtime evidence model covering uptime, deployment, backup, and recovery records
- support, escalation, and recurring review standards

## Initial Success Condition

The initiative will have succeeded at this first stage when the enterprise has a reviewable service catalog and runtime evidence model that can govern operational claims across operations, environment support, and hosting without overstating present operating maturity.
