## 1. Purpose

This document defines the minimum considerations for developing action plans after capability gaps are reported. It ensures that post-evaluation planning is practical, risk-informed, and aligned to the closed-loop ECEGF governance model.

## 2. Scope

This guidance applies whenever a capability gap has been reported and the finding requires remediation planning, mitigation, formal deferral, or explicit risk acceptance.

## 3. Planning Objective

The objective of action planning is to convert a reported gap into a managed decision with clear ownership, priority, timing, and expected outcome.

## 4. Minimum Planning Considerations

For each reported gap, planners should consider:

| Consideration | Required Planning Question |
| --- | --- |
| Gap definition | What exactly is not meeting the intended standard? |
| Business impact | What happens if the gap remains unresolved? |
| Root cause | What is most likely driving the gap? |
| Control or capability objective | What improved state should the action achieve? |
| Priority | How urgent is the issue relative to other work? |
| Ownership | Who is accountable for delivery and review? |
| Dependency | What tools, approvals, funding, or upstream work are needed? |
| Feasibility | Can the action be delivered within current constraints? |
| Residual risk | What risk remains even if the action is completed? |
| Validation | How will success be measured and confirmed? |

## 5. Action Decision Paths

Each reported gap must be dispositioned into one of the following paths:

| Disposition | Use When | Required Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remediate | The gap should be corrected now | Action owner, due date, and expected result defined |
| Mitigate | Full correction is not immediately practical | Interim control or workaround documented |
| Defer | Action is valid but intentionally delayed | Reason, review date, and approval recorded |
| Accept Risk | Gap will remain by decision | Rationale and approving authority recorded |

## 6. Minimum Action Plan Content

An action plan should include:

- Reference to the originating capability gap or report
- Clear action statement written as a deliverable outcome
- Accountable owner
- Priority level
- Target due date
- Dependencies or prerequisites
- Success measure
- Required approval, if applicable

## 7. Prioritization Guidance

Use the following priority logic for MVP governance:

| Priority | General Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| High | Significant operational risk, core capability impact, or urgent control weakness |
| Medium | Meaningful inefficiency or inconsistency with manageable short-term exposure |
| Low | Improvement is beneficial but can be scheduled without near-term material impact |

Priority decisions should consider both impact and practicality, not severity alone.

## 8. Review and Approval

- Capability Owner reviews the proposed plan for practicality and ownership.
- Governance Lead confirms alignment with framework standards.
- Approver confirms disposition for high-risk actions, deferrals, and risk acceptance decisions.

## 9. Review Cadence

| Activity | Cadence |
| --- | --- |
| New action plan review | At time of gap disposition |
| In-flight action review | Biweekly through the improvement register |
| Deferred or accepted risk review | At least quarterly |

## 10. Required Inputs and Outputs

| Inputs | Outputs |
| --- | --- |
| Capability gap report | Approved action plan or disposition |
| Evidence and impact statement | Priority and ownership assignment |
| Delivery constraints and dependencies | Register entry and review date |

## 11. Approval

This guidance is approved for use as the baseline action-planning standard for the NickLinney.Ops governance MVP. Material deviation should be documented and justified.
