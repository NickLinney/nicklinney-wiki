## Purpose

This document packages the RCMF vs. SICAF capability gap analysis into an executive action plan. It translates observed gaps into approved priorities, practical workstreams, decision points, and review cadence so that NickLinney.Ops can move from strategic intent to governed operational execution.

## Inputs

This action plan is derived from:

- The RCMF vs. SICAF capability gap assessment
- Current operating constraints and delivery dependencies
- The need to establish a minimum viable governance baseline before broader optimization

## Executive Position

SICAF defines the direction of travel, while RCMF reflects how work is currently being executed. The immediate need is not to redesign strategy, but to tighten the operating model so that controls, decisions, data, and accountability consistently support that strategy. The first phase should therefore focus on governance clarity, evidence discipline, and assurance routines that make performance visible and defensible.

## Action Planning Method

Actions are organized according to four principles:

- Stabilize governance before scaling process change
- Fix evidence and reporting before pursuing broad automation
- Address high-impact dependencies first
- Assign one accountable owner per action, even where execution is shared

## Prioritization Logic

| Priority level | Decision criteria |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate | Required to establish accountability, control confidence, or executive visibility |
| Near term | Strengthens repeatability and reduces execution variability |
| Planned | Builds on the stabilized baseline to improve efficiency and scalability |

## Recommended Workstreams

| Workstream | Objective | Expected outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governance baseline | Formalize decision rights, approvals, and escalation rules | Clear accountability and faster, more defensible decisions |
| Control and evidence model | Define standard control ownership, evidence requirements, and retention | Consistent proof of execution and easier assurance |
| Process standardization | Normalize critical workflows and closure criteria | Reduced variation and better operational resilience |
| Management reporting | Implement a concise KPI/KRI and exceptions view | Better executive oversight and earlier issue detection |
| Assurance cadence | Establish review, issue tracking, attestation, and closure testing | Sustained improvement and stronger control discipline |

## Decision Criteria

Actions should be approved when they:

- Resolve a high or moderate gap with clear evidence
- Improve the reliability of decision-making or control execution
- Have a practical owner and achievable delivery path
- Strengthen the baseline for later standardization or automation

## Management Outputs

The minimum required outputs from this plan are:

1. Approved governance baseline.
2. Current action register with accountable owners.
3. Control inventory with evidence standards.
4. Standard monthly executive report pack.
5. Assurance review log and issue closure record.

## Summary Recommendations

1. Treat governance clarity and evidence discipline as the first-order fixes.
2. Standardize critical workflows before expanding automation or tooling complexity.
3. Use recurring executive reporting and assurance reviews to convert the framework into an operating routine.
4. Reassess after the first 180 days to confirm whether the baseline is stable enough for the next maturity phase.

## Conclusion

The minimum viable governance framework for NickLinney.Ops should be simple, evidence-based, and durable. If the actions above are approved and governed consistently, RCMF can become an operational model that reliably expresses SICAF intent rather than approximating it through informal practice.
