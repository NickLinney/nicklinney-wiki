## Purpose

This guide defines the minimum viable method for evaluating enterprise capabilities through the Strategic Intent and Capability Architecture Framework (SICAF). SICAF is used to assess whether a capability is intentionally designed, strategically aligned, and architecturally coherent before deeper operational analysis is performed.

## Scope

SICAF applies to business, technology, operational, and cross-functional capabilities that materially affect enterprise direction, investment, control, or delivery outcomes. It is used for new capabilities, major changes, periodic health reviews, and pre-investment governance decisions.

## Core Position

SICAF evaluates strategic architecture and intent. It asks whether the enterprise has clearly defined what the capability is for, how it should work, how it fits the target operating model, and what good looks like.

## Principles

- Evaluate design intent before execution maturity.
- Favor evidence over narrative opinion.
- Assess the capability as designed at the enterprise level, not as experienced in one team only.
- Separate structural weaknesses from temporary delivery issues.
- Write conclusions that executives can act on without translation.

## Evaluation Domains

| Domain | Evaluation focus | Typical questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic Alignment | Business purpose and outcome fit | Is the capability tied to defined strategic goals and decisions? |
| Capability Definition | Boundaries, services, and ownership | Is the capability clearly described, owned, and distinguishable from adjacent capabilities? |
| Operating Model Fit | Roles, decision rights, and interfaces | Does the intended operating model support accountable execution? |
| Process Architecture | End-to-end design and control points | Are the major workflows and governance controls intentionally designed? |
| Information Architecture | Data, records, and reporting intent | Is required information defined, governed, and usable for oversight? |
| Technology Enablement | Platform and tooling design intent | Do systems and integrations support the target state? |
| Measures and Policy | Standards, KPIs, and policy anchors | Are success measures and governing rules explicit? |

## Evidence Expectations

Acceptable evidence should be current, attributable, and reviewable. Preferred evidence includes:

- Strategy statements, business cases, and decision records
- Capability maps, service definitions, and ownership models
- Operating model diagrams, RACI artifacts, and governance forums
- Process maps, control catalogs, and exception paths
- Data definitions, reporting packs, and metric dictionaries
- Architecture diagrams, application inventories, and integration maps
- Policies, standards, and approved procedures

Interviews may clarify evidence but should not replace documentary support where formal design claims are made.

## Scoring Approach

Use a five-point scale for each domain.

| Score | Interpretation | Decision meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Undefined | Strategic intent is absent or materially unclear |
| 2 | Emerging | Intent exists in fragments but is inconsistent or weakly governed |
| 3 | Defined | Core design is documented and reasonably coherent |
| 4 | Managed | Design is complete, governed, and decision-useful |
| 5 | Optimized | Design is integrated, measurable, and consistently improved |

Apply scores using the preponderance of evidence. A high score should require both documented intent and visible governance mechanisms. If evidence is mixed, score to the lower defensible level and note the constraint.

## Workflow

| Step | Activity | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Confirm evaluation scope, capability boundary, and sponsors | Assessment charter note |
| 2 | Collect and index evidence | Evidence register |
| 3 | Review domains against available artifacts | Working assessment notes |
| 4 | Calibrate scores and key findings | Draft scorecard |
| 5 | Identify strategic risks, dependencies, and design gaps | Issue summary |
| 6 | Prepare the SICAF Assessment Report | Executive-ready report |
| 7 | Present results for governance review | Approved findings and follow-up actions |

## Deliverables

- Completed SICAF domain scorecard
- Evidence register with source references
- Executive summary of strengths, gaps, and implications
- SICAF Assessment Report

## Review Cadence

- At capability inception or major redesign
- Before material funding or transformation decisions
- Annually for critical enterprise capabilities
- Ad hoc when a major risk, merger, restructuring, or platform shift changes strategic assumptions

## Reporting Expectations

The companion SICAF Assessment Report should be concise, standardized, and executive-friendly. It should describe strategic posture, summarize evidence-based scores, identify design gaps, and recommend decisions or follow-up analysis without excessive technical detail.
