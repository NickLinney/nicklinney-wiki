# Executive Retrospective Authoring Brief

## Brief Purpose

This brief defines the required structure and evidentiary discipline for the executive retrospective set that follows the `Enterprise Capability Investigation`.

## Required Inputs

Each executive retrospective must draw from:

- the `Enterprise Capability Report to the Executive Chair`
- the `Enterprise RCMF vs. SICAF Capability Gap Report and Action Plan`
- the assigned module-level `RCMF vs. SICAF Capability Gap Report and Action Plan` artifacts
- the `Realization Rebaseline Register`
- any directly relevant supporting investigation artifacts needed to explain a conclusion

## Required Sections

Each executive retrospective must contain the following sections:

1. Executive Perspective and Role Basis
2. Assigned Module Set
3. Reading of the Enterprise Capability Investigation
4. Module-by-Module Reasoning from the `RCMF vs. SICAF Capability Gap Report and Action Plan` corpus
5. Cross-Module Observations and Dependencies
6. Top Action Item Proposal for the Upcoming Fiscal Year
7. Action Plan
8. Expected Gap-Closure Effect on Next Year's ECI

## Action Item Rules

- Each executive may nominate only one top action item.
- The action item may take the form of a project, product, improvement, feature, framework, or standard.
- The action item must be specific enough to govern and assess later.
- The action item must be justified against the assigned module set rather than against a vague enterprise preference.

## Action Plan Minimum Content

Each action plan must identify:

- the problem condition being addressed
- the bounded objective
- the primary module or modules expected to improve
- the key evidence objects that would prove higher realization next year
- major dependencies or prerequisites
- principal risks
- the expected effect on RCMF position if successful

## Tone and Interpretation Rule

The retrospectives are interpretive executive documents. Unlike the director-led cabinet report, these artifacts may include executive judgment, prioritization, and strategic interpretation. Even so, judgments must remain anchored in evidence and must not overstate realization.
