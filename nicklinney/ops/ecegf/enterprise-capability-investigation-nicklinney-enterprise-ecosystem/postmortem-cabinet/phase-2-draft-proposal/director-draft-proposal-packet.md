# Enterprise Capability Investigation Postmortem Cabinet - Phase 2 Director Draft Proposal Packet

## Packet Purpose

This packet assembles the director draft sections prepared after Phase 1 cabinet action. It serves as the working proposal base for the final refinement phase.

## Draft Proposal Convergence

Across all five directors, the draft proposal converges on six governing points:

1. The realization re-baseline remains controlling for all report language.
2. The enterprise is strategically substantive but operationally under-realized.
3. The only presently defensible narrow operating footholds are `NickLinney.Ops`, `NickLinney.env`, and `NickLinney.KnowledgeManagement`.
4. Architecture, security, documentation, and delivery findings should all be anchored first in enterprise synthesis, then in module-level artifacts.
5. Registry and dependency claims remain weaker than the design body suggests.
6. The final report should emphasize evidence-backed gap amelioration and strategic advancement without broadening scope beyond the current investigation.

## Director Draft Sections

The architecture draft section states that the enterprise’s primary architectural problem is not absence of design but insufficient realization evidence across architecture-critical modules. It identifies `NickLinney.System`, `SyntheOS`, `NickLinney.Modules`, and `NickLinney.DataInfrastructure` as priority architecture-governance subjects because each is strategically meaningful, high-severity, and still not realized. It further argues that dependency mapping and sequencing should be governed by realization evidence rather than conceptual adjacency.

- The enterprise is strongly designed but only narrowly realized.
- The realization re-baseline must remain controlling throughout the report.
- Most cross-module dependency claims remain non-authoritative unless realization evidence is cited.
- `NickLinney.Ops`, `NickLinney.env`, and `NickLinney.KnowledgeManagement` are the only presently defensible narrow operating footholds.
- Architecture-critical, security-critical, documentation-critical, and delivery-critical findings all depend on improved evidence discipline rather than scope expansion.
