## Enterprise Condition in Summary

The cabinet reports that the enterprise is strategically substantive, strongly designed, and operationally under-realized. Across 21 assessed modules, the enterprise synthesis records an average SICAF position of `2.95 / 5` and an average re-baselined RCMF position of `1.14 / 5`. Only three modules are presently evidenced as `Realized in Narrow Scope`: `NickLinney.Ops`, `NickLinney.env`, and `NickLinney.KnowledgeManagement`. The remaining 18 modules must be treated as `In Progress / Not Realized`.

These three narrow-scope footholds are meaningful for governance, environment maintenance, and documentation traceability, but they do not extend realization proof into adjacent modules.

## Significant Gaps

- The enterprise has a large realization gap between strategic design maturity and proved operational maturity.
- Most cross-module dependency claims remain weaker than the design body suggests and must be treated cautiously unless direct realization evidence is cited.
- Security-specific operational proof remains insufficient despite meaningful design intent.
- Registry and documentation controls remain materially dependent on modules whose governed runtime state is still not proven.
- The current narrow operating footholds do not provide authority to infer realization across the broader enterprise.

## Recommended Paths of Gap Amelioration

The cabinet does not recommend scope expansion or a new intervention program at this stage. It recommends the following bounded paths of amelioration, each drawn from the existing investigation logic:

- Preserve the realization re-baseline as the non-negotiable operating assumption for enterprise reporting and planning.
- Use the module-level `RCMF vs. SICAF Capability Gap Report and Action Plan` set as the controlling enterprise evidence body.
- Require named ownership, evidence expectations, and review obligations wherever high-severity modules are treated as active planning dependencies.
- Build recurring proof packs from the narrow operating footholds while explicitly tracking the non-realized status of the remaining modules.
- Keep dependency claims, registry claims, hosting claims, and security readiness claims qualified until direct realization evidence materially improves.

## Recommended Paths of Strategic Advancement

The cabinet recommends a strategic advancement path that remains realization-constrained and evidence-first:

- advance by proving bounded capability slices rather than by broadening enterprise claims
- advance by clarifying ownership and governance cadence before treating conceptual dependencies as active runtime dependencies
- advance by using recurring proof packs and reassessment cycles to decide when a module may legitimately move from `In Progress / Not Realized` to a stronger realization status
- advance by preserving the current design body as target-state intent while refusing to let target-state coherence substitute for current-state proof
